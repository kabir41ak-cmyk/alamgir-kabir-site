Beyond the headline

A journey shaped by lived experience.

This is not a story about a perfect path or a finished success. It is an account of learning, adapting, working alongside others and continuing to ask whose voices are missing.

What guides the work

Rights before charity. Participation before representation. Accessibility as a condition for equality—not a favour.

I grew up moving between places in search of stability. When my father was seriously injured while working, financial responsibility came early. I worked as a paperboy and missed academic years while helping support my family.

Those experiences made the realities of childhood, poverty and exclusion impossible to treat as abstract issues. I became involved in child-led spaces and community initiatives because I wanted children to have the safety, choices and opportunities that every child deserves.

I was born without a right hand. I include this as part of my lived experience—not as a request for sympathy, and not as the whole story. It has strengthened my conviction that access, accommodation and attitudes determine whether people can participate on equal terms.

Today, my work connects community-based child rights efforts with inclusion, safeguarding, youth participation and humanitarian practice in Cox’s Bazar.

In the field and on stage

A principle, not a slogan

“A person’s opportunity should not depend on whether they fit the system. Systems should become accessible to people.”

I am interested in the practical work of making that principle real—in teams, services, communities and institutions.
